5 San Diego golf courses every visitor can play

A PGA Tour clifftop muni, an Arnold Palmer resort design, and a bayfront bargain — five San Diego rounds every visitor can actually book.

Greenskeeper Journal · September 14, 2026 · 5 min read
IN THIS GUIDE1. Torrey Pines Golf Course — South Course2. Aviara Golf Club3. Maderas Golf Club4. Omni La Costa Resort & Spa — North Course5. Coronado Golf Course

San Diego might have the deepest bench of visitor-friendly golf on the West Coast: a municipal course that hosts the PGA Tour, resort layouts from golf's biggest names, and one of the best value munis in the country — all within about 40 minutes of the airport. Here are five rounds that belong on any first-timer's shortlist, each one bookable, each one distinct, and each one worth keeping a memory of.

1. Torrey Pines Golf Course — South Course

The South Course at Torrey Pines is the rare layout where you can play the exact course the pros play on a regular PGA Tour stop. This municipal facility, owned and operated by the City of San Diego, sits on coastal cliffs above the Pacific in La Jolla. The original 36 holes were designed by William P. Bell and William F. Bell and opened in 1957; Rees Jones rebuilt the South in 2001 and refined it again in 2019. It hosts the Farmers Insurance Open every January and has hosted two U.S. Opens, in 2008 and 2021 — the South stretches past 7,700 yards for tournament play, one of the longest tests on Tour.

This is the round to plan your trip around. Tee times are in high demand, so book as far ahead as the booking window allows and consider the North Course — Tom Weiskopf's 2016 redesign, flipped so the ocean views fall on the finishing holes — as a worthy second round at the same facility. Play from tees that match your game; the rough and 82 bunkers punish vanity. If you can't get the South, even a sunrise walk on the North with the cliffs and reserve below is a trip-maker.

2. Aviara Golf Club

North of the city in Carlsbad, Aviara is the only coastal California course designed by Arnold Palmer — and the only Palmer design in the San Diego area. Opened in 1991 as part of the Park Hyatt Aviara resort, the par-72 layout winds through rolling hillsides above the Batiquitos Lagoon nature preserve, landscaped with native wildflowers, waterfalls, and water features. It was the longtime home of the LPGA's Kia Classic, drawing the best fields in women's golf every March.

As a resort course it is open to the public, which makes it the easiest premium tee time in the north county. It pairs naturally with La Costa, just a few miles away, for a Carlsbad 36-hole day — or with Torrey Pines for a north-coast one-two. The layout is more playable than it looks at 7,007 yards from the tips, with wide fairways and four sets of tees, but the fast, sloping greens reward a practiced short game.

3. Maderas Golf Club

Head inland to Poway and the scenery changes completely: rolling foothills, rock outcroppings, and more than forty acres of native wildflowers. Maderas, a Johnny Miller and Robert Muir Graves design opened in 1999, is a semi-private club that welcomes daily-fee visitors — Troon-managed, firm and fast, and widely considered the closest thing to a private-club experience the public can buy in San Diego. Golf Digest ranked it among the country's best public courses from 2013 through 2016.

This is the course for golfers who want a strategic, target-oriented test rather than a coastal postcard. Elevation changes and forced carries demand course management, so bring an honest set of tees. Book in advance — weekends go quickly — and plan it mid-trip between the coastal rounds, since it's only about 30 minutes from downtown. The Tuscan-style clubhouse and practice facilities are among the best in the area, so arrive early.

4. Omni La Costa Resort & Spa — North Course

La Costa's tournament pedigree is unmatched in the county: the resort hosted 37 elite PGA Tour events, including the Tournament of Champions from 1969 through 1998, won by Nicklaus, Player, Watson, and Woods. The North Course — formerly the Champions Course, originally a 1965 Dick Wilson design — reopened in 2024 after a full rebuild by Gil Hanse and Jim Wagner that widened fairways, raised greens, and converted hazards to native barranca. The NCAA Division I men's and women's championships now call it home.

Resort guests get the most reliable access, so if La Costa is your base, it solves both lodging and golf. The North is the championship test; the South Course (formerly Legends) is the more forgiving, tree-lined complement, and a North-South double is one of the area's best 36-hole days. Because the renovation is recent and the collegiate spotlight is growing, this course's profile is rising — play it now before tee sheets get even tighter.

5. Coronado Golf Course

Finish the list with one of the best value rounds in America. This 1957 municipal design by Jack L. Daray sits on a quiet peninsula across the two-mile Coronado Bridge from downtown, with several holes playing directly along San Diego Bay and views of the skyline and the historic Hotel del Coronado. At 6,590 yards it's compact, flat, and walkable — but the 78.5 rating and 138 slope say it still has teeth, especially the closing stretch of 16, 17, and 18 along the water.

Coronado is where the day's logistics matter most: half the tee times go through a day-of-play lottery and city residents get booking preference, so out-of-towners should plan to call a couple of days out and be ready for a standby list if the lottery doesn't break their way. Pair it with the Hotel del Coronado for the full island experience. It may be the cheapest round on your trip — and possibly the most memorable.
