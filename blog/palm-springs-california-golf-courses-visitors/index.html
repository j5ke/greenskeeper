5 Palm Springs golf courses every visitor can play

Five Coachella Valley rounds open to visitors: Pete Dye's famous Stadium Course, a former PGA Tour host, city-owned munis, and one of the best public 36-hole setups in California.

Greenskeeper Journal · September 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Greater Palm Springs is one of the densest collections of resort golf in America, with the Coachella Valley's mountain-framed layouts drawing winter golfers from everywhere. The good news for visitors: many of the best courses here are genuinely public, including a PGA Tour host and two city-owned layouts that punch far above muni expectations. These five are all bookable by anyone, and together they cover every kind of desert golf trip.

1. PGA West Stadium Course, La Quinta

Designed by Pete Dye and opened in 1986, the Stadium Course is the marquee public layout in the desert: a par-72, 7,300-yard test from the tournament tees that serves as the host course for the final round of the PGA Tour's American Express each January. Dye shaped spectator seating into the natural terrain — the reason for the 'Stadium' name — and the closing stretch is pure theater: the 617-yard par-5 11th nicknamed 'Eternity,' the par-5 16th with its deep pit bunker, and the island-green par-3 17th, 'Alcatraz,' set in the middle of a pond. Golf Digest ranks it among the toughest courses in America and one of California's top public plays.

Tee times at the Stadium book up fast in peak season, and many visitors deliberately time their trip for late January to play it in near-tournament condition. This is a serious test, so pick an honest tee box — the white tees at just over 6,100 yards are plenty for most golfers — and pair it with the Nicklaus Tournament Course on the same property for a two-day Dye-and-Nicklaus comparison. If you're only playing one round in the desert, this is the one to build the trip around.

2. Indian Wells Golf Resort — Celebrity Course, Indian Wells

The municipal resort at Indian Wells offers 36 holes open to all visitors, and the Celebrity Course — designed by Clive Clark and opened in 2006 — is the prettier of the two, nicknamed 'Beauty' for its cascading water features, wildflower displays, and corridors framed by palms and mountain views. It plays 7,050 yards to a par of 72, with narrow, shot-making corridors rather than brute length, and it hosted the PGA Tour's LG Skins Game twice. Both Indian Wells layouts sit in Golfweek's Top 25 Best Municipal Courses in America — the only 36-hole facility in California that can say that.

The Celebrity is the friendlier, more scenic round, which makes it a natural opener before tackling the John Fought-designed Players Course — 'The Beast' — for a 36-hole day at one property. Multiple tee options keep it playable for higher handicaps, and the resort partners with neighboring hotels for stay-and-play packages. No housing lines the holes; each hole feels tucked away, which is rare for desert resort golf.

3. La Quinta Resort & Club — Mountain Course, La Quinta

The oldest of the public courses at PGA West, the Mountain Course was Pete Dye's 1980 original for the historic La Quinta Resort — a layout that helped turn the desert into a resort-golf mecca and earned spots on national top-100 lists. It winds right up against the Santa Rosa Mountains, with pot bunkers, rock formations, and elevated tee boxes that trade the Stadium Course's spectacle for rugged scenery. The highlight stretch is 14 through 17: the par-5 15th wraps around craggy slopes, and the signature par-3 16th drops from a high tee to a green set in a moonscape of boulders.

Because it shares Dye's DNA with the Stadium but plays shorter and more forgiving, the Mountain Course works perfectly as a warm-up round the day before you take on the Stadium. It shares a clubhouse with the Dunes Course, so a 36-hole day across both is easy to arrange, and staying at the resort — which dates to 1926 — puts you steps from the first tee. Book tee times through the resort's golf desk and request an afternoon round for the best mountain light.

4. Desert Willow Golf Resort — Firecliff Course, Palm Desert

Owned by the City of Palm Desert and open to everyone as a daily-fee facility, Desert Willow is proof that desert 'munis' can rival private clubs for conditioning. The two 18s were designed by Michael Hurdzan and Dana Fry on a flat desert floor, with enough earth moved to create holes that frame the surrounding mountains. Firecliff is the tougher test: over 100 bunkers, extensive natural areas, and water features woven through a championship-length layout that has drawn environmental-design recognition. The sibling Mountain View course is slightly gentler but plays to a similar standard.

Firecliff is arguably the best value in the valley for the quality — a genuinely challenging design without resort pricing or private-club gates. Plan a 36-hole day across both courses: open with the friendlier Mountain View, then take on Firecliff once you're warmed up. If you're traveling in October, check the overseeding schedule before booking, since the courses can close briefly for it. The Terrace Restaurant on site makes a solid 19th hole.

5. SilverRock Resort — Arnold Palmer Classic Course, La Quinta

Owned by the City of La Quinta, SilverRock is an Arnold Palmer design that debuted in 2005 and immediately entered the PGA Tour rotation, hosting the American Express from 2008 to 2011. The Classic Course stretches 7,239 yards across more than 200 acres, defined by massive native bunkers and water features set against the Santa Rosa Mountains. A re-routing in 2017 refreshed the layout as part of the property's ongoing resort development, and its practice facility is considered one of the best in the desert — Cobra rental sets are available if you're traveling light.

SilverRock is the smart pick for golfers who want a Tour-tested layout at muni-style access: book directly through the resort's website, where tee times require prepayment. Note the fall overseeding closure if you're planning an autumn trip, and pair the round with an evening in La Quinta's Old Town Village, a short drive away. It also works well as the 'value anchor' day between pricier rounds at PGA West and Indian Wells.
