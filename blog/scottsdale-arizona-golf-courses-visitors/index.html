5 Scottsdale golf courses every visitor can play

From a PGA Tour stadium course to desert tracks routed around granite boulders — five visitor-friendly rounds in the heart of Arizona golf country.

Greenskeeper Journal · September 09, 2026 · 4 min read
IN THIS GUIDE1. TPC Scottsdale — Stadium Course2. We-Ko-Pa Golf Club — Saguaro3. Troon North Golf Club — Pinnacle4. Grayhawk Golf Club — Talon5. Talking Stick Golf Club — O’odham

Scottsdale might be the easiest golf trip in America to plan: dozens of standout courses, nearly all of them open to visitors, and everything within a short drive. But “open to the public” covers a lot of ground, from Tour venues to quiet desert tracks. Here are five rounds that belong on any first-timer’s shortlist — each one bookable, each one distinct, and each one a story worth keeping.

1. TPC Scottsdale — Stadium Course

This is the only course on the list where you can stand on a par-3 tee with a stadium around you. The Stadium Course has hosted the WM Phoenix Open since 1987, and the 16th — a full coliseum of a par 3 — is the most famous hole in desert golf. The Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish design is more than a spectacle, though: the back nine is a genuine risk-and-reward test with water in play on six holes.

Treat this as the centerpiece round of your trip rather than a casual add-on. Peak-season tee times go early and the course plays very differently from the tips than from the forward tees, so pick a set that matches your game and leave the ego at home. Check current booking windows and policies before you go — this is the one Scottsdale round worth planning around.

2. We-Ko-Pa Golf Club — Saguaro

Twenty-five minutes east of Scottsdale, on Fort McDowell Yavapai Nation land that will never be developed, sits one of the best public courses in Arizona. The Saguaro, a Bill Coore and Ben Crenshaw design, is everything desert golf should be: wide, walkable fairways tracing natural washes, 360-degree views of four mountain ranges, and not a house in sight. It has ranked among Golf Digest’s top public courses in the state for years.

Pair it with the Cholla, the club’s original Scott Miller design, for a 36-hole day — regulars split their preference between the two almost evenly, so play both and settle the debate yourself. Book the earliest tee time you can stand; the desert light on the first few holes is half the experience.

3. Troon North Golf Club — Pinnacle

If you want the classic postcard version of desert golf, this is it. Troon North’s two Tom Weiskopf courses wind through the foothills beneath Pinnacle Peak, where giant granite boulders — some the size of houses — sit directly in play. The Pinnacle’s signature par-3 16th, a tee shot over water to a sprawling green, is one of the most photographed holes in the state.

This is the sternest test on this list, with severe elevation changes and forced carries, so choose your tees honestly. The payoff is a round that feels like nowhere else on earth. Golfers who fall for it tend to add the Monument course the next day for the full 36-hole Troon North experience.

4. Grayhawk Golf Club — Talon

Grayhawk has been 100 percent public since it opened in 1994, and the Talon course is its wilder half. The David Graham and Gary Panks design skirts thick Sonoran desert before diving into a back nine routed around a series of deep box canyons — steep drop-offs, dense mesquite and ironwood, and large tiered greens that demand attention to the day’s pin sheet. It once hosted the finals of the Andersen Consulting World Championship of Golf.

Talon rewards positioning over power; several holes look tighter from the tee than they play. It pairs naturally with the club’s Raptor course, a Tom Fazio design with more generous fairways, for a two-round day. The clubhouse scene is one of the liveliest in Scottsdale, so linger after.

5. Talking Stick Golf Club — O’odham

A second Coore and Crenshaw design makes the list, but a very different one. Where the Saguaro at We-Ko-Pa is rugged desert, the O’odham at Talking Stick is traditional golf in a desert setting: generous fairways, strategic bunkering, and subtle green complexes on a walkable, relatively flat piece of ground. It’s the “player’s course” of the club’s two layouts — the more you play it, the more you like it.

Make this your first round of the trip. It’s forgiving off the tee while you shake off the travel rust, easy to reach in north Scottsdale, and an ideal warm-up before the sterner tests above. The sister Piipaash course, a tree-lined parkland-style layout, makes for a pleasant 36-hole contrast in the afternoon.
