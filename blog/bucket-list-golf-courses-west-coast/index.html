5 West Coast golf courses for your bucket list

From the Monterey Peninsula to the Oregon dunes, five courses to build a future golf trip around.

Greenskeeper Journal · September 09, 2026 · 3 min read
IN THIS GUIDE1. Pebble Beach Golf Links, California2. Spyglass Hill, California3. Bandon Dunes, Oregon4. Pacific Dunes, Oregon5. Old Macdonald, OregonTurn five names into one realistic trip

A useful golf bucket list is more than a collection of famous names. It should help you decide what kind of round you want to experience, what belongs on the same trip, and what deserves a journey of its own. These five West Coast picks are an editorial shortlist chosen for their contrasting settings, rather than a numbered ranking of quality.

1. Pebble Beach Golf Links, California

Start here if your dream round revolves around a coastal setting and championship history. Pebble Beach puts the Monterey Peninsula shoreline at the center of the experience.

Treat this as the anchor of a trip rather than another stop in a packed itinerary. Before arranging flights, compare your preferred dates with the resort’s current booking requirements and leave room to enjoy the destination beyond the round.

2. Spyglass Hill, California

Spyglass Hill offers a different visual character, moving between sand and tall pines. It makes a natural comparison with Pebble Beach for a golfer who wants two distinct course settings on one Monterey trip.

Choose it for variety rather than treating it as a substitute. Keep your itinerary flexible enough to recover between rounds, especially if you plan to walk.

Explore Spyglass Hill ↗

3. Bandon Dunes, Oregon

Bandon Dunes belongs on a list built around links golf and the Oregon coast. The resort’s collection lets you plan several rounds around one destination.

For a first visit, make a short list of priorities before requesting dates. Booking a trip around the courses you most want to see is more useful than filling every available hour with another round.

Bandon Dunes course collection ↗

4. Pacific Dunes, Oregon

Pacific Dunes adds coastal dunes and a different routing to a Bandon trip. It is a strong candidate when the landscape is as much a part of your bucket list as the name on the scorecard.

Put it next to Bandon Dunes in your planning notes and record what you want from each round. Allow for changing weather, and check the resort’s guidance on clothing, walking, and caddies before you travel.

Explore Pacific Dunes ↗

5. Old Macdonald, Oregon

Old Macdonald draws inspiration from golf architect Charles Blair Macdonald. It adds an architecture-focused reason to explore the Bandon collection.

Choose this stop if you enjoy thinking about the different ways a hole can be played. After the round, write down a hole that changed your mind; that memory can be more meaningful than the final score.

Turn five names into one realistic trip

Split this list into two possible journeys: Monterey and Bandon. Choose one destination first, then check course availability, accommodation requirements, travel time, and your total budget. Avoid assuming that a public or resort course always has an easy booking path.

These selections are inspiration, not a promise of availability or an official ranking. Before committing money, confirm the current details directly with each property.
