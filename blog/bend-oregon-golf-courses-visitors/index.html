5 Bend golf courses every visitor can play

Five visitor-accessible rounds around Bend, Oregon: a David McLay Kidd brute, a Bob Cupp heathland woven through two rivers, the state's only public Nicklaus signature course, a Deschutes River canyon shotmaker's track, and a red-lava-bunker charmer in Sisters.

Bend sits at the heart of one of the best golf corridors in the American West — high-desert golf where fescue fairways run fast, ponderosa pines line the fairways, and the Cascade peaks show up in the background of nearly every tee box. Every course below is genuinely open to a traveling golfer: no member invitation needed, just a tee time (and in one case, a resort room). Together they cover the full Bend experience, from the headline championship test to the relaxed morning round in a mountain town half an hour away.

1. Tetherow Golf Club, Bend

The marquee course in Bend. Designed by David McLay Kidd — the architect behind the original Bandon Dunes — and opened in 2008, Tetherow is a public course built on a manufactured landscape of lumps and bumps, heavy bunkering, a couple of lakes, and fescue fairways and greens that give tee shots plenty of roll. Golf Digest has ranked it among America's 100 Greatest Public courses since 2011, sitting at 61st for 2025–'26, and it was as high as 57th for 2023–'24. The same page warns it's a bear of a test — even Kidd admits it can be too penalizing — but at its best it rivals anything in the state.

This is the round to build the trip around. Book well ahead, especially for summer weekends, and plan on an early tee time so the afternoon winds don't add a club to every approach. Tetherow is a big-ball, target-golf course, so bring a confident driver and leave the ego at the wrong tee box — the forward tees make it playable for mid-handicaps while still keeping the scenery. Pair it with a recovery round at Widgi Creek or Aspen Lakes later in the trip rather than back-to-back with another championship test.

2. Crosswater Golf Course at Sunriver Resort, Sunriver

About 15 miles south of Bend, the Crosswater course at Sunriver Resort is the classic championship track of central Oregon. Designed by Bob Cupp and opened in 1995, it's a traditional Scottish heathland layout threaded by the Deschutes and Little Deschutes Rivers — depending on your tee selection, the rivers can come into play as many as seven times in a round. It's set on 600 acres of woodlands and protected wetlands, was named an Audubon-certified sanctuary in 1999, and hosted the JELD-WEN Tradition — one of the senior majors — from 2007 through 2010. Tee times go to members and Sunriver Resort guests, so visitors get on by booking a stay-and-play package.

Because access comes through the resort, this is the anchor for a stay-and-play itinerary. Sunriver is a full resort with two other courses — the John Fought–designed Meadows and the Robert Trent Jones Jr.–designed Woodlands — so a two-night stay gives you three distinct courses plus lodging and dining on property. Book the resort package early in the season and take the Crosswater round on day two, after Meadows has loosened you up for the rivers and heathland carries. If the Fazio/Nicklaus courses across the valley are the headline tests, Crosswater is the more traditional championship experience of the five.

3. Pronghorn Club — Jack Nicklaus Signature Course, Bend

The resort now called Juniper Preserve holds the nationally recognized Pronghorn Club and its two courses, and the Jack Nicklaus Signature is the one any visitor can play — the resort's own guide calls it the only fully-public Nicklaus course in Oregon, designed in the desert-style tradition. Golfweek's 2026 ranking of every state's best public-access courses places it 10th in Oregon, and it's built for golfers of all skill levels: fast, firm conditions, lava-rock ridges, and Cascade views. The Tom Fazio Championship course next door is members-only (public access is limited to occasional special offers), so the Nicklaus course is the one to target.

The Nicklaus Signature works best as a mid-trip headline round. Tee times are available to the public, but the resort suggests staying at Juniper Preserve for the full experience — booking directly through the resort secures the preferred times and opens up the stay-and-play side of things. It's the most forgiving of the big-name Bend tests, with generous landing areas and playability for higher handicaps, so pair it with Tetherow for a contrast: desert-style golf one day, fescue linksland the next. Book through the resort rather than a third party to get the package rates.

4. Widgi Creek Golf Club, Bend

A few miles from downtown Bend, Widgi Creek is the shotmaker's favorite — a semi-private club that welcomes public play. Designed by Robert Muir Graves and opened in 1991, it winds through towering ponderosa pines along the rim of the Deschutes River canyon: tight, tree-lined fairways, some of the best-maintained greens in the area, and water in play on several holes. Golf Digest called it 'the sleeper in Central Oregon,' and Golf Magazine called it a 'must play' — the course where local knowledge matters and the tree-lined routing rewards positioning over power.

Widgi Creek is the ideal morning round: it's close to town, the conditions are famously sharp early, and it's less of a physical grind than Tetherow or the Nicklaus course. Book it as a warmup before the headline courses or as the easy bookend after one. It's the best pick of the five for a precise-but-not-long ball-striker — if you keep it in the short grass you'll score, and if you spray it you'll be punching out of ponderosas all day. The Grill on-site makes it an easy lunch-and-round combo.

5. Aspen Lakes Golf Course, Sisters

About 25 minutes west of Bend in the old-west town of Sisters, Aspen Lakes is the locals' favorite and the most visually distinctive course on the list. It's a public, family-owned course designed by William Overdorf that plays toward views of the Three Sisters peaks — and it's famous for its red bunkers: the course ground the local volcanic cinder into the traps instead of shipping in white sand, giving the whole layout a striking rust-red look. The bunkers play differently than regular sand (they're coarser and pack down), which the locals are quick to tell you is part of the charm.

This is the trip's reset button. Book Aspen Lakes for the morning after a championship-test day — the parkland layout is friendlier, the pace tends to be relaxed, and the mountain views do half the work. Pair it with a stroll through Sisters' western-style shops and a brewery lunch back in Bend, or make it the easy arrival-day round if you're flying into Redmond. High handicaps will find it the most approachable of the five, and it's the one course every Bend local insists a visitor hasn't done right without playing. Ask the starter for the local line on the red bunkers before you tee off.
